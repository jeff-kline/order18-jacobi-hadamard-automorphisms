# Field rigidity for the order-18 Jacobi Hadamard matrices

Candidate research note, 17 November 2026

Let $d$ be positive and odd, $E=\mathbb F_{343^d}$, $Q=|E|$, and $H$ the completed order-18 matrix in its published presentation. Its row and column indices each consist of four border points and four separate copies of $E$. We allow all independent signed row and column permutations. An equivalence between two copies of the construction means an equivalence of their completed arrays after identifying their defining field, characters and mask convention. An arbitrary relabeling transports the recovered structures to the new indices.

**Theorem.** The ordinary matrix determines the border, the four field blocks as an unordered partition on each side, and the zero of each block. In the given coordinates every automorphism fixes all eight border indices, fixes each block individually, and acts on all eight copies of the field by the *same* map
$$
x\ \longmapsto\ a x^{343^j},\qquad a\in (E^*)^{18},\quad j\in\mathbb Z/d.
$$
Thus the effective group has order $d(Q-1)/18$. For the full group of pairs of signed permutations, take the direct product with the order-two group generated by simultaneous negation. The coordinate identifications retain the scalar and Galois ambiguity displayed in the formula.

**Base case.** At order 1,376 these are exactly 19 effective actions and 38 signed actions. An independent exhaustive search through normalized bipartite graphs used 32 possible anchor images, individualization and refinement, and a check of the actual entries on every accepted leaf. The 19 solutions are the simultaneous scalar multiplications. [Base enumeration](base/aut.cpp) · [Search transcript](base/group-output.txt)

## 1. Which quartet is the border?

Use the previous proof that the only four-row sets with at most four minority columns are the border rows $B$ and the four field-zero rows $Z$. Their minority sets are the border columns and the four zero columns, respectively. For four signs the minority is defined up to negating their whole product, so the exceptional columns are invariant under all allowed equivalences. [Array, quartet proof and finite base certificate](../research-directions-2026-11-17/jacobi-proof.md)

Partition columns by their pattern on the unordered eight-row union $B\cup Z$, up to global sign. Its nonsingleton cells all have size $(Q-1)/6$ and each is a union of three order-18 classes in one field column block. Choose one of the two candidate quartets $T$. Its projective sign patterns group those 24 fine cells into four groups of six. For a row $r$ outside $B\cup Z$ and a fine cell $C$, fold the residue of $\sum_{c\in C} H_{rc}H_{sc}$ modulo 37 up to sign into $\{0,\ldots,18\}$. The anchor $s\in B\cup Z$ can be chosen arbitrarily because every two such rows are proportional on $C$. For each group of six cells record its six folded values in increasing order. The set of six-tuples that occur over all outside rows and groups is an invariant of the choice of $T$.

For each possible $d\bmod36$ there is a tuple realized with $T=B$ and absent with $T=Z$. For example $d=1$ gives $(1,3,5,5,7,7)$. This finite certificate labels the true quartet at every degree. To verify it, form the 24 cells from the signs of the four border and four zero rows, and enumerate the $4\times18$ possibilities for the outside row block and nonzero class. A cell sum uses only $T_d(i,j)=\#\{z\in C_i:1-z\in C_j\}$. Over $\mathbb F_{37}$ this is the $d$-fold convolution of $T_1$ plus the constant $(343^d-2-341^d)/324$, as proved by the elementary Euler-product argument in the earlier note. This reduces *all* positive odd degrees to 18 exact checks. [Certificate that prints a witness for each degree class](quartets.cjs)

On the identified border rows, projective column patterns recover the four field column blocks and four singleton border columns. Conversely, on those border columns the field rows have projective patterns from the four columns of $U$, by the lower-left block $-U^T$, and the border rows are already known. This recovers the field row blocks. The other exceptional quartet and its four minority columns mark one zero in each of the eight field blocks. An equivalence acts on each field block with constant entry sign, by evaluating its field rows on a border column and its field columns on a border row. A second certificate identifies the same border using the Paley submatrix instead of six-tuples. [Independent frame proof](algebra/rigidity.md)

## 2. Recovering a graph on each field block

Take two different rows in one field block. Color the pair by the ordered four inner products of the rows restricted to the four field column blocks, reduced modulo 37. Their signs are preserved since row signs are constant on a block and column signs cancel. Reordering the field column blocks permutes the four components in the same way for all pairs.

In field coordinates these four numbers are the four periodic autocorrelations of the masks at the difference of the two row coordinates, up to a fixed permutation of the four entries. A reversal of the difference has no effect. The same description holds for two columns using the four field row blocks. Compute each correlation at an index $e$ by summing $T_d(i,j) f(C_{i+e+9}) f(C_{j+e})$; the two omitted endpoints cancel. At every one of the 18 degree classes, each vertex has three colors of valency $(Q-1)/9$ and three of valency $2(Q-1)/9$. The union of the three smaller colors consists precisely of differences with index $1\bmod3$. The verifier checks the signed residues and the precise class set for every degree. An equivalence must therefore preserve the graph whose differences belong to this one cubic coset. Multiplication by a fixed nonzero scalar identifies it with the graph of nonzero cubes. The following elementary lemma completes the field-theoretic step. The conclusion about this graph also follows from Lim and Praeger, Theorem 1.2(4). [Generalised Paley graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605252)

## 3. An elementary cubic-graph lemma

Let $p$ be a prime congruent to 1 modulo 6, $q=p^r$, $D=(p-1)/3$, and $m=(q-1)/3$. In the undirected graph on $\mathbb F_q$ with edges defined by nonzero cubic differences, every automorphism is $F(x)=a x^{p^j}+b$, with $a$ a nonzero cube.

To prove this, first show that an automorphism preserves the three values of $(x-y)^m$ separately. Edges give the value 1. For a nonzero noncube $t$, let $N(t)$ count common neighbors of 0 and $t$. In the field, put $P(y)=1+y^m+y^{2m}$. On nonzero arguments this is three times the indicator of cubes. At $y=0,t$ the other factor vanishes, giving
$$
9N(t)=\sum_y P(y)P(t-y)=-2-\binom{2m}{m}t^m\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_q.
$$
Indeed only the powers $y^{q-1}$ survive summation, with sum $-1$, since $4m<2(q-1)$ and the constant sums to zero. The two products of degrees $m,2m$ give coefficient 2. The product of degrees $2m,2m$ contributes the displayed binomial term, since $m$ is even. The base-$p$ digits of $m$ are all $D$, those of $2m$ are all $2D<p$, and the coefficient is $\binom{2D}{D}^{\,r}\ne0\bmod p$ by expanding in Frobenius powers. The two nonedge values of $t^m$ therefore have different common-neighbor counts even modulo $p$.

It follows that $(F(x)-F(y))^m=(x-y)^m$ for all $x,y$. Let $S$ be the space of polynomial functions spanned by $x^i$ whose base-$p$ digits are at most $D$. The rows of the kernel $(x-y)^m$ span exactly $S$. Its binomial coefficients are nonzero precisely for those indices, and the complementary monomials in the other variable are linearly independent as functions of degree below $q$. Hence composition by $F$ preserves $S$.

Represent functions by their polynomials of degree below $q$. The function $G=F^{p^j}$ and its first $D$ powers belong to $S$ for each $0\le j<r$. In particular $\deg G\le m$. Inductively $\deg G\le m/k$ for $1\le k\le D$, because $k\deg G\le km/(k-1)\le2m<q$ at step $k\ge2$, and $G^k\in S$. Therefore $\deg G\le m/D$, which has all digits 1. Multiplication by $p$ on positive exponents modulo $q-1$ rotates their digits. The constant term stays constant. Rotating any digit to the leading position shows that every exponent in the support of $F$ has only digits 0 or 1.

Accordingly $F$ is multi-affine in the formal variables $X_j=x^{p^j}$. If its support depends on two variables, choose different rotations $G=F^{p^j},\,H=F^{p^k}$ both depending on $X_0$. Then $G^D H\in S$ because $Dp^j+p^k$ is an allowed exponent. As a formal polynomial it has degree exactly $D+1$ in $X_0$ and degree at most $D+1<p$ in each variable. Substitution of the Frobenius powers gives distinct reduced exponents without any carry. Thus a nonzero monomial with digit $D+1$ survives, contrary to membership in $S$. A permutation is nonconstant, so precisely one variable occurs and $F=a x^{p^j}+b$. Testing on an edge forces $a$ to be a cube. For a graph defined by a scalar multiple of the cubes the same semiaffine conclusion follows by conjugation. This proof concerns an established graph automorphism result. The research contribution is extracting the graph from the completed Hadamard matrix.

## 4. Synchronization and the exact group

As each field block has a marked origin, its map is additive, with zero translation. Write $P_i,S_j$ for the four row and four column maps, and $\pi,\rho$ for the respective block permutations. In a core cell the argument of the mask is $\pm(y+t_{ij}x)$, where $t_{ij}=-1$ on the diagonal and $+1$ elsewhere. Hold $y+t_{ij}x$ fixed and vary $x$. The target mask must be constant along every coset of the image of
$$
t_{\pi(i),\rho(j)}P_i-t_{ij}S_j.
$$
Each target mask sums to 1, so it has no nonzero additive period: such a period would group its terms into $p$-cycles. The displayed operator vanishes. This forces all eight maps to be the same semilinear map up to eight fixed coordinate signs.

Only a field-independent calculation remains. Enumerate the permutations of four border rows, four field row blocks, four border columns and four field column blocks. The entries at borders and field zeros determine their entry signs up to simultaneous negation, leaving 96 possibilities out of $24^4$. The $t$ identities leave 48. For each, the shared map acts on class indices by $e\mapsto h+7^k e$ modulo 18, with 18 choices of phase $h$ and three of $k\bmod3$. Check all class signs in the 16 core cells. Exactly one normalized possibility preserves the array. It fixes all four permutations and has $h=0$, $k=0\bmod3$, and positive coordinate signs. Every map in the theorem conversely preserves the defining entries. This argument uses the surjective class labels in $E$ and applies unchanged when $3\mid d$. ## Audit

Three independent approaches checked the distinction between the quartets or the base group. The base graph search compares actual entries, the first uniform calculation compares cell sums and the second detects the Paley submatrix. The cubic graph proof was reconstructed and challenged separately from the design-theoretic proof. The statement determines the coordinate maps of the given tower. It leaves open a universal rigidity theorem for unrelated cyclotomic Hadamard constructions. These are local research results with no human specialist review or global priority determination.

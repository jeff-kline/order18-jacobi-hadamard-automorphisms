# Fourteen classes on the characteristic-7 tower

Let $Q=343^d$ with $d$ positive and odd. In the second family write $(A,B,C,D)$ for the four masks in the order given in the [two-family theorem](theorem.md), with $D$ the Paley mask. Put $\bar A(x)=A(-x)$. The two completed matrices with placements $(\bar A,B,C,D)$ and $(\bar A,B,D,C)$ are inequivalent under independent signed row and column permutations. Each has full effective automorphism group of order $3d(Q-1)/18$. Together with their transposes, the even placement $(A,B,C,D)$ and its transpose, and the eight classes of the first family, they give **at least fourteen ordinary equivalence classes at every order $4(Q+1)$ in this tower**.

In both odd placements the only row quartets with at most four minority columns are the border quartet and the four field-zero rows. Both have the same four minority border columns. For $Q>343$ this follows from the [uniform character bound](theorem.md). Exchanging the mask roles preserves the Fourier-support and mean estimates. At $Q=343$, exhaustive pair-product enumeration checks both placements and their four minority columns using the field $\mathbb F_7[t]/(t^3+t^2+1)$ and the generator $t+2$. Both primitive character orientations are included. Powers of 7 account for the remaining primitive characters.

Mark either row quartet, delete it and its four minority columns, and partition the remaining rows and columns by their projective signs on the deleted columns and rows respectively. There are four parts on each side. The true border marking gives four $Q$ by $Q$ Paley submatrices in which all absolute scalar products of distinct rows equal 1. For the other marking, each row part consists of one original border row and the nonzero field rows of one original block. Each column part consists of one field-zero column and eighteen nonzero class atoms. For every one of the sixteen resulting submatrices, the [certificate](odd-frames.cjs) exhibits a pair consisting of its border row and an ordinary row whose restricted scalar product is different from $\pm1$ modulo 37. Thus an ordinary equivalence between the two matrices must send the true border to the true border.

For completeness, let $T(i,j)$ count nonzero $z$ in multiplicative class $i$ with $1-z$ in class $j$, both taken in the base field. Fourier transformation on $(\mathbb Z/18)^2$ identifies these coefficients with Jacobi sums. Odd norm lifting raises nontrivial Jacobi coefficients to their $d$th powers. Transforming back gives the $d$-fold convolution of $T$ plus a constant table with entries $(343^d-2-341^d)/324$. All calculations can be performed in $\mathbb F_{37}$, which contains a primitive eighteenth root. Each positive-degree power then depends only on $d\bmod36$. The certificate checks the eighteen odd residues, the two placements and the two character orientations. It checks every local border-row versus field-row test at $d=1$ separately by summing the entries in the actual column part. It also verifies each of the sixteen failing predicates and the nonflatness of the three non-Paley masks.

With the border distinguished, the [marked-placement argument](../algebra/placements.md) applies. The zero row in each field block is marked by the other exceptional quartet, even though its minority columns coincide with the border. A Paley block gives a genuine design isomorphism after normalization; its sign is determined by its row sum. Under Kantor’s theorem the affine point and block maps on that design have paired translation terms, so preserving the zero row forces both translations to vanish. The trivial-period argument synchronizes the eight coordinate maps. The finite [transporter calculation](../algebra/placements.cjs) then excludes opposite permutation parities. It gives three normalized self-transporters for each odd representative. They extend to $3d(Q-1)/18$ distinct effective automorphisms, and the border recognition proves that the enumeration is exhaustive.

The pair of odd placements therefore gives two distinct ordinary classes. Each has no closed row quartet and exactly one closed column quartet. The even placement has no closed row quartet and two closed column quartets. Transposes of these three classes have the two ordered counts interchanged, producing three more classes. Each of these six has at least $3d(Q-1)/18$ effective automorphisms, whereas all eight classes from family I have exactly $d(Q-1)/18$. This proves the lower bound.     node ../algebra/placements.cjs --second 1246 1264
